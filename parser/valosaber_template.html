Valosaber

Baseball learned to ask what a player actually added to winning, once context and luck are taken out. This page asks the same of one Valorant match, using the numbers hidden in the replay.

From the diamond to the round

Sabermetrics replaced counting stats with a few ideas. Each one has a Valorant version that this replay can support, and two that it cannot yet.

The leaderboard

Click a column to sort. WPA is the share of rounds a player won for their team through kills, minus the share they gave away through deaths. Index columns read 100 as the match average.

Utility-adjusted credit

A kill is not all the killer's doing. Here each kill's win probability is shared with the players who set it up. Move the sliders to see how much the rankings depend on the split. The shares always add back to the kill's value, so nobody is credited twice.

Healing credit turns HP restored into the win probability of the death it helps prevent. Set it to 0 to switch it off. Setups with no matching effect hand their share back to the killer.

How far to trust it

  • The model is simple on purpose. It assumes every living player is equally likely to get the next kill, and it ignores the spike, the clock, weapons and position. Real round win probability would be fitted to many matches. This is the theoretical version, like the first run-expectancy tables.
  • One match is a small sample. Five rounds and 34 kills. A 173 finishing index on two kills is noise, not skill. Baseball analysts regress small samples toward the average before drawing conclusions, and that needs more replays.
  • Two baseball ideas are missing. Replacement level needs a pool of players to compare against, and park factors need many matches per map and side. Both come with more data.
  • Left out for lack of data. Economy is recorded but every value is zero in this match. Assists exist but their type codes are undecoded. Headshot regions are not mapped to names.
  • Utility credit rests on assumed weights. Only 7 of 34 kills had a recorded utility setup, and ability times are accurate to about two seconds, so an effect within eight seconds before a kill counts. The default 60/25/15 split is a starting guess. With more matches the weights can be measured from how often affected enemies actually lose fights.
